## Joy & Curiosity #107

寫程式已不再足夠，要有生活與商業經驗，才有東西值得做成軟體。

**Why** 過去一份點子要花十倍的寫程式力氣，現在 AI 讓比例趨於平衡，剩下的問題是該拿這些產能做什麼。

**How** 作者借 Herzog 的 Rogue Film School 類比，主張要讀商業、市場與軟體如何被使用與販售，也要去工地、餐廳、書店等沒人談軟體的地方工作，找到打造軟體的理由。

**What** Register Spill 的 Joy & Curiosity #107（今年最後一期，一月回歸）。另提到作者團隊推出 Amp 的 Boxes（就是一台 VM，上面跑著 agent）、Levent Alpöge 宣稱證明黎曼猜想、GTA VI 24 小時賣出 37.9 億美元，以及 Ben Thompson 的 everflux software 與 translucent sources 概念。

### 重點
- 核心論點：AI 讓寫程式與點子的比例趨於平衡，所以要靠商業理解與非軟體領域的生活經驗找到值得做的東西
- Boxes：就是一台 VM，可請 Amp 部署多個 app，每台 box 上有 agent 可直接協助編輯 production 內容
- Ben Thompson 兩個概念：everflux software（軟體持續演進而非版本間隔發布）、translucent sources（閉源軟體因 LLM 逆向而近乎開源）

In Werner Herzog’s Rogue Film School , he taught his pupils how to pick locks or forge signatures . Herzog says the reason behind that curriculum is that some films can’t be made if you don’t “ have the right criminal energy and the skills ”. (His movie Fitzcarraldo , whose production Wikipedia calls “troubled” only to then mention that a 320-ton steamship had to be pushed over a hill in the Amazon rainforest and that, at the end of filming, the chief of a native tribe told Herzog they could, you know, take care of the problem for him and kill Klaus Kinski, that movie couldn’t have been made, in Herzog’s words, without “massive forgery”.) Now, that’s what Herzog says as to why you need criminal energy and “the skills”. My interpretation of the curriculum is different. And yes, I’m going to use my criminal energy here to re- and possibly misinterpret the founding idea behind a school I neither visited nor was involved in. I think that he’s teaching how to pick locks and forge signatures and be rogues because you need to live a life , ideally a full life, in order to make films. Because if you don’t have that, what are you going to make films about? About school? About learning how to make films? No, you have to have something worth sharing, something worth talking about: experiences, ideas, opinions, doubts, delights, heartbreaks, and questions. And I think it’s the same for programming now. Yes, it’s always been the case that programming alone, the typing of code and the construction of computer programs, wasn’t enough. But there was so much typing, and syntax, and libraries, and function signatures, and all that stuff to wrestle with that the ratio was off: for one bit of idea you had to spend ten bits of programming. Now it’s much more balanced and you have to figure out what to do with all the programming you can now accomplish. So, if I had to build a Rogue Software School, I’d get my students to learn about business, about startups, about marketing, about markets, about the places where software is made and sold; I’d want them to learn about how software is used and why, learn about the trends involved in all of that, learn how to market and sell software. And I’d also want them to spend time in places where nobody talks or even thinks about software. Work on a construction site, unload trucks, help in a restaurant, work in a bookstore, run a youth soccer club. Find something that gives you a reason to build software, because programming isn’t enough anymore. Note: This will be the last Joy & Curiosity this year. I’ll be back in January. You can tell Amp now to deploy something to a box and it’ll do it. You can have as many apps as you want running on a box. Apps, daemons, scripts, whatever you want. It’s just a computer. There are no containers or other isolation. Agents absolutely love that. I have a tiny box on which I run my newsletter-link collector app, my metronome app to practice drums, a festival store app for my local club, and a bunch of other things. Best of all: on each box there’s an agent running. No longer do I have to carefully edit text or other content or data in production. No, I can just point the agent at it via the built-in widget and say “format this nicely” and it’ll do it. Jellyware! Bad ideas that aren’t bad anymore .
