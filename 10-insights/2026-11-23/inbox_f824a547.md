The gadgets I find most interesting these days aren’t phones, laptops, or watches. Many don’t even have a screen. They just improve an everyday object. Here are five I’m finding useful, from a digital highlighter and headphones that measure my focus to a quiet alarm clock and a smart ball. Subscribe now Share Mark II — A Handy Digital Highlighter The Mark II is a digital highlighter that lets me scan and save passages from anything I’m reading. I drag it over my favorite lines in a book or magazine. Those highlights are automatically tagged and searchable in the Mark app, which is free to use with the highlighter. I also sync what I save to Readwise , which I use for organizing my highlights, including my favorite Kindle book passages and what I’ve saved from podcasts with the Snipd app. It’s become my central hub for everything I’ve clipped. I can query it from my AI tools like Claude and ChatGPT. In the past, I used my phone to scan book passages with the Readwise app. That worked well but required me to interrupt my reading with a screen. I appreciate that the Mark II feels more like a traditional highlighter than a phone. It has a useful voice note option: I can press a button to record my thoughts about whatever I’m reading. And it comes with a slim magnet that keeps it in your book. It’s not perfect, even if Time listed it as one of the Best Inventions of 2026 . I’ve often had to fill in words the Mark II skipped over. The app doesn’t signal that it might have missed words, so I’ve had to check passages on my phone for accuracy. And it’s slow to run over one line at a time. For a long passage that runs across multiple pages, the Readwise app is faster. Still, I like the Mark II’s design and I’m continuing to use it as a screenless way to save great sentences. Price: $159 is what I paid. It’s now on sale for $149. Neurable One — Headphones That Sense When I’m Fading These headphones have built-in sensors that assess my level of concentration. Like an EEG machine, they pick up electrical signals from my brain. In my testing, the readings generally lined up with how alert or tired I felt. When I’m doing deep work and my attention drops, the headphones play a chime. It’s a useful nudge to take a break. The accompanying app features games for practicing sustained attention. You can also use it to run experiments to see which behaviors deepen your focus, like going for a brisk walk or writing down a list of tasks you’re holding in your head. The headphones are comfortable and sound good for listening to podcasts or music. They don’t match my Sony XM6 headphones for noise canceling or battery life. I like using this Neurable headset, though, to see how long I can stick to a task before my mind wanders. It’s helped me notice when I’m sharpest during the day. Price: $449 on sale at the moment. $499 normally. No subscription cost. I recently found Uncommon Sense Teaching and added it, along with Jay Yang’s You Can Just Do Things. Shortform also helps me revisit books I read a long time ago. Its guides are written and edited by actual humans. The analysis goes beyond the surface, explaining how the book I’m reading differs from other popular books on the same subject. I like that I can send a preview of a book to my Kindle, or download a PDF. Wonder Tools readers get a free trial and 49% off the annual plan. Try Shortform Free Loftie — A Gentler Way to Wake Up This alarm clock and sound machine wakes me up with soft chimes and a small glowing panel of lights at the bottom of the clock. I’d rather hear its plucked strings at 7:00 a.m. than a barrage of electronic beeps. When I’m reading before bed, I use Loftie ’s rain or campfire sounds to block out New York City noise. There’s also a playlist of meditation sounds, and you can use it as a Bluetooth speaker. You can even design your own bedtime sounds with the app, though I haven’t tried that. My favorite feature: At night I can turn the screen off completely to keep the room dark. To check the time, I tap the snooze button. Hold down the button and a night light comes on. You can set a different alarm time for each day, or have it silence all alarms on weekends. You can also set a “go to bed” alarm to avoid staying up late. Price: $199. No subscription cost. It’s an elegant device, but a costly one. Cheaper alternative : I bought and recommend this $14 Peakeep Alarm Clock . Its screen also turns off completely, and it’s small enough to fit in a pocket. It has no Bluetooth, playlists, or meditation features, but it’s simple and reliable. MOFT — A Flexible Phone Stand When I read or watch videos on my phone, I use this stand to keep my phone at an ideal angle without having to hold it. I’ve used pop-out sockets as phone grips in the past, and finger ring holders. The MOFT unfolds into several positions, so I can raise the phone higher when I’m recording. It also has a slot for holding a credit card, though I don't use it for that. My model is the Snap Phone Tripod Wallet AERA. It sticks to my phone case with MagSafe magnets. My case is a simple, durable one from Smartish . I don’t add charms, stickers, cords, or other flair. I like the simplest model that just protects my phone. The MOFT is sturdy and flexible, but not cheap. Price: $50 for the MOFT I have. $20 for the Smartish case. Share Wonder Tools Ball2 — A Ball That Keeps Score Sensors inside this grapefruit-sized ball track how fast or high it goes, and how much it spins. The free app on iOS or Android lets you design and play solo or group games. It shows you live stats like ball height, speed, spin, and airtime. I’ve enjoyed more than a dozen games with my daughters so far, including: Catch and volley games. Pass the ball as many times as you can without dropping it. The app keeps track of your streaks. Roll and shake . Roll the ball as far as you can, along a designated path, or to a specified distance. Or see who can shake it fastest. Creative challenges. In Water Balloon Toss, you try to catch the ball softly (sensors measure the force with which your hands meet the ball) or it “pops” and you lose that round. In Limbo Toss , each successive throw and catch has to be lower than the previous one, starting with as high a throw as possible. It’s fun designing new challenges together. Years ago I clutzily broke a lamp playing catch indoors with a balloon. So we’re mainly using this ball outdoors, where there’s more room. If you have a big basement, that works too.
