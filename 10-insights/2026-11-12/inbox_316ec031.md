A Fortune 100 retailer put out an RFP for an ad server and didn't like any of the answers. Then they found AdButler online. Rajiv Khaneja had never sold to an enterprise, and AdButler had 12 people. He showed up at the retailer with a laptop and a demo, and signed his first Fortune 100 customer. 

 Rajiv explains how AdButler sells enterprise deals with no salespeople: inbound leads from a contact form, product-led demos, missing features built in weeks, and month-to-month contracts instead of lock-in. He also shares the pricing mistake he made for 15 years. 

 Plus: why he thinks AI will kill the settings page and give every customer their own version of your software. 

 Rajiv started AdButler as a teenager after DoubleClick hung up on him. It ran as a side project for a decade, with plans from $9.95 a month, before he went all in and built it into a $10M SaaS. 

 🔑 Key Lessons 
 
 Let the demo be your sales process: AdButler has no salespeople. Product people show the software screen by screen, and Rajiv watches the room to see what excites buyers. That demo won the retailer and AdButler's next enterprise deals. 
 Answer the gap before the buyer asks: At the end of every demo, AdButler shows how it will fill whatever is missing. Promising three weeks against a competitor's six months, then shipping in two, won long-term enterprise contracts. 
 Price on value, not on your costs: For 15 years, Rajiv charged a multiple of what it cost to serve an ad. His first enterprise deal was many orders of magnitude bigger than his SMB plans. 
 Don't lock customers in with contracts: AdButler offers enterprise clients month-to-month terms and signs longer ones only when asked. If a contract is the only thing keeping a customer, Rajiv says you've already lost them. 
 Put an enterprise door on your website: After that first deal, AdButler added an enterprise tier with a contact form. Its first 10 enterprise customers all came through inbound leads, with no outbound sales team at all. 
 Split features into one-person pieces: AdButler breaks every big feature into the smallest units one engineer can ship alone, like single API endpoints. Fewer people syncing means fewer meetings and much faster custom builds. 
 Treat AI like the tractor next door: Rajiv tells his team the farmer with a tractor sells carrots cheaper than the one with an ox. Founders who won't adopt AI will get priced out the same way.
