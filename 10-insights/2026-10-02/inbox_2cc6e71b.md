## AI Agents for Teachers

教師可用 Claude Cowork 設定代理工作流，但需留意代理迴圈的三種失敗方式。 **Why** 作者指出多數教育科技的 AI 代理目前在後台或管理端運作，教師很少能直接掌控，而 Claude for Teachers 被他視為教師端代理使用的第一個突破。 **How** 作者說明 Cowork 網頁版透過 Google Drive、OneDrive、Slack、email 等雲端連接器運作，桌面版則處理本機檔案與資料夾；建議把含個資的教材放入單一資料夾，只授權 Claude 存取該資料夾。 **What** 文中提供兩個工作流：依 scope and sequence 規劃學期單元，以及掃描 Drive 中既有教材後重建單元；作者列出代理迴圈的三種失敗：幻覺被放大、把一次對話即可完成的工作變成迴圈（造成 token 成本），以及缺乏外部檢查（如程式的測試套件）。

### 重點
- Cowork 網頁版透過雲端連接器存取 Drive、OneDrive、Slack、email；桌面版主要處理本機檔案
- 代理迴圈三種失敗：幻覺會在迴圈中層層放大、誤用迴圈造成 token 成本、缺乏外部檢查（如測試套件或驗證器）
- 工作流範例：先以 Cowork 搜尋 Drive 指定資料夾的單元教材，再要求 Claude 診斷限制並提出後續教學設計

You could ask a model to book an appointment, set up an Uber ride, or purchase something on Amazon, and then watch it stumble through the test. You may remember McDonald&#8217;s pulling its drive-through agent after the order system added bacon to ice cream and hundreds of chicken nuggets to a customer&#8217;s bill. An agent, I wrote, is still a tool, and one that depends on other agents, namely human beings, to coordinate and maintain efficacy, efficiency, and ethical orientation. In other words, the marketing overstepped the functional reality. In an era still promising AGI by 2027, the word agent carried a techno-utopian charge: benevolent machines handing over limitless leisure time to its operators. I didn&#8217;t buy into this marketing then, and I don&#8217;t buy it now. What Changed in 2025 and 2026 But a lot has changed in late 2025 and 2026. The McDonald&#8217;s story is now two years old. Agents are now being used to automate critical aspects of coding projects, streamline financial and sales processes, and engage in deep analytical work that is determining strategy in major corporations around the world. There is no longer a question about the efficiency and value of this AI functionality. Meanwhile, education is stuck in a debate about AI literacy, tool implementation, cognitive offloading, and tech backlash. The gap is only widening between education&#8217;s 2024 understanding of AI and what these systems can really do. How Piad Morffis Defines an Agent According to Alejandro Piad Morffis , architect of the first Cuban language LLMs, an agent is &#8220;just a software component that acts autonomously, interacting with a (computational) environment and constantly making choices to further some [goal].&#8221; He continues, &#8220;The difference between traditional software and agent-based software is that, in a traditional software, all changes to a system are in response to some user input . . . In agent-based software, the software itself is active in the sense of making adjustments and producing effects . . . regardless of whether the user is there.&#8221; Three Ways the Loop Fails To continue to lean into Piad Morffis&#8217;s work , agentic loops fail in three ways different from an AI chat. First, agentic loops amplify hallucinations. A user can catch and reroute a misleading sentence in a single chat cycle. In an agentic loop, hallucinations cascade into faulty belief structures and analytical frameworks that can lead to serious missteps in action and evaluation. Second, the loop fails when the user mistakes a need for an agentic process, and a single chat would have sufficed. Here, failure primarily takes the shape of inefficiency and token cost. Third, a loop with no external check has nothing to tell the system when it has worked or fallen short. In other words, the model is deciding for itself when it has completed reasoning. In a coding environment, the check is the test suite. In deployment cycles, the check is a verifier that has ingested specific metrics. Without this layer, an agentic loop operates with an insufficient amount of human input and oversight. T Where Agents Already Work in Ed-Tech As I stated in a recent article , ed-tech companies are right now designing products that bring AI agents closer to classroom instruction. First, AI agents work in the background, assisting ed-tech companies in building platforms, tracking sales, deploying and implementing product, marketing, building training, and measuring impact. Here, I provide this long list to indicate that business continues to discover new applications for agentic AI, even as doomsayers and bubble-bursters declare these tools impracticable, ineffective, and unprofitable. Second, the platforms themselves run many agentic processes under the surface UX. Any element of automation may have an attending agentic process. For example, accurate and up-to-date data dashboards are only possible because of finely-tuned agentic and generative processes. Third, some AI toolmakers keep agentic processes working behind the scenes, some like Panorama , Infinite Campus , and Otus see the agentic aggregation of school data as their primary moat. Here, generative AI translates large batches of data into recommendations for impactful, timely action. Claude for Teachers as the First Breakthrough What should be obvious from this list is that most agentic processes are currently working behind the scenes or from inside the admin suite. Classroom instructors can take advantage of agentic processes through various ed-tech platforms, but always at a distance or through some carefully calibrated intermediary. Claude for Teachers represents the first breakthrough in teacher use of agentic AI. The platform comes with ample reassurances about data privacy and safety . Some districts are allowing teachers and staff to open accounts and even download Claude Cowork or the Claude browser . That said, the small subset of schools that approve of Claude for Teachers are not allowing desktop and browser functionalities. It is my sense that this will remain the state of play until Claude offers a more responsive admin dashboard that allows tech directors, etc., to observe, organize, and manage agentic workflows by individual teachers. Now that we have landed on Claude for Teachers, I want to provide a quick how-to guide for agentic setup and the deployment of low-level tasks like organization, forms generation and management, and lesson or unit planning. Cowork on the Web and on the Desktop Claude Cowork has a web and desktop version. If your district doesn&#8217;t allow the download of the desktop version, you still have access to agentic processes inside the web version of Claude. To understand the difference here between web and desktop versions: think of different modes of access to different kinds of documents, media, etc. The web version works through cloud connections to Google Drive, OneDrive, Slack, email, etc. You will need to give Claude permission to access these applications. The desktop version works primarily with local files and folders, and provides the most control over agentic workflows. Note that this work structure&#8211;either through cloud connectors or API&#8211;makes tech directors very nervous. I have written about this in a previous post on Claude for Teachers . Again, until tech directors have finer controls on these connectors, districts will continue to keep these functionalities out of reach for most teachers. But let&#8217;s say you are in a district that allows such experimentation, or you want to set this up on your own computer. Here is how you do that: Setting Up a Simple Workflow At this point, I want to offer up my version of step-by-step instructions for setting up a simple agentic workflow for teachers, even if you can only dabble with this on your personal computer. What follows are two workflows built in Cowork that I have chosen because a single prompt or prompt iteration usually does not produce the best results. Planning a Unit Against Your Scope and Sequence The first is semester-long unit planning against a scope and sequence, standards, or HQIM. As you have already probably seen, a single prompt can write a unit plan or even a year-long plan of instruction. Let&#8217;s say you want to chart a course through a literature-based 11th grade English class focused on a few target texts. It is possible to do so through a single prompt or prompt iteration, but the content is usually pretty bad. The build lacks deep context and persistent memory across tasks or engagements. When you want to modify a single unit or rebuild a module in your course, you have to feed back the context into the next chat, losing precious details in the process. You can set up a standalone Project to get somewhat better results, but project spaces also involve a certain level of inflexibility, bound to the uploaded files and lacking crossover &#8220;memory&#8221; to chats outside the project space. By contrast, Cowork allows you to convert a project, for instance, into an ongoing loop. It gives you more control of the loop&#8217;s operation, the contextual materials, and ultimately the outputs. As already noted, there are products on the market that enable these loops but out of the view of the user. A teacher can chat with the platform to mix up the agentic loop on the backend, but true teacher agency lies in orchestrating the loop more concretely and explicitly. A Source-and-Standards Sweep Across Your Drive The first is a source-and-standards sweep across your own Drive. Most teachers have years of accumulated material scattered across Drive, old units, worksheets, slide decks, article PDFs, primary sources, none of it tagged by standard, most of it forgotten. When you plan a new unit, you rebuild from scratch because you cannot remember what you already have, or find it if you do. Here is the decision point: Do you want to manually load the relevant docs into Project&#8217;s files or link them in small batches through the chat add files features, or do you want Claude to have wider visibility into your instructional archive? Admittedly, there are risks to allow Claude access. What if some of your docs in your Drive include PII? If this is the case, I would recommend moving all the related docs into a folder, and only giving Claude access to that folder. And then moving forward, organize your folders with this functionality and its limitations in mind. It is my sense that this functionality is going to push us all to be better curators of our files and content. So after that long preamble, here is my loop. First, I set up a Project focused on a specific course or task. In an &#8220;11th Grade ELA Semester 1&#8221; Project, with Drive connected to the relevant folders, I start with something plain: &#8220;Search [folder name] in Drive for any materials related to the unit I am about to teach on [topic], readings, slides, worksheets, assessments, PDFs.&#8221; To activate this agentic activity, you only need to toggle to Cowork in the regular chat. Claude will then access the target materials. Now you follow up with more specific directions about what you want Claude to do with the relevant files. &#8220;Sketch out an alternative version of a first unit with new factors in mind.&#8221; &#8220;Diagnose the limitations of the past materials, and make suggestions for subsequent instruction builds.&#8221; &#8220;Redesign this course in light of my school&#8217;s new commitment to brain-based education, or its response to the tech backlash.&#8221; Note that this is a fairly limited agentic process. We would close the loop more agentically if we could ask Claude to create a file in the designated folder with its analysis or new materials, but that functionality is not activated in most Claude for Teachers accounts right now. For the moment, Claude does the reading and the building, and you copy over what is worth keeping into a new documents in the targetted folder.. Deliberation Is the Step That Matters Let&#8217;s remember that the most important step in the agentic process is deliberation. In the process above, we are giving Claude the space to make decisions about what is relevant to the task at hand, about what counts as a limitation, about how a course should bend toward a new commitment.
