About the book

When Ethan Mollick wrote Co‐Intelligence , working with AI meant prompting a chatbot that couldn’t do basic math. A short time later AIs are winning gold at the International Math Olympiad, reshaping professions from programming to medicine, and helping authors make really nice looking web sites for their new books.

Co‐Existence is a field guide to this moment, written in, and about, a time before true AGI. With the warmth, humor, and deep research that made his last book a bestseller, Mollick draws on the latest data and real‐world cases to answer the questions everyone is actually asking:

This is a book about how to co‐exist with AI that is sometimes, but not always, better than you —about how good it has become, and where it still falls short.

The idea at the center of the book

Imagine a map of how hard a task is for a person. The further from the center, the harder it gets.

Now build a wall around the center. Inside is what AI can do well; outside is what it can’t. You might expect a smooth circle, with the easy things inside.

Instead, the frontier is wildly jagged. Tasks that seem trivially simple sit outside it, while tasks that look hard fall well within.

And it keeps growing, but not evenly. Nobody can hand you the map for your own work, so part of co‐existing is learning to draw it yourself.

Examples from the book. Inside the frontier, though hard for people: a Math Olympiad gold medal, poetry that passes for human, legal briefs as good as an expensive attorney’s, and political arguments tailored to one person. Outside it, though easy for people: reliably counting objects in an image, staying consistent across a long conversation, and knowing when to say “I don’t know.”

The other big idea in the book

AI has been on an exponential curve, with its capabilities doubling every few months.

Up close, each doubling feels like a small step. The writing is a bit sharper; there are a few fewer mistakes.

But every doubling is bigger than all the progress before it combined, so everything behind you flattens into the floor.

That is why so many AI plans are built around the tools of today rather than the curve we’re on.
