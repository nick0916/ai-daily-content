## AI Prompts I Keep Coming Back To

八組可反覆套用的 AI 提示詞，用來理清思路、挑戰假設、規劃與改稿。

**Why** 作者認為不需要收集上百個提示詞，幾個簡單的就能涵蓋日常大部分任務。

**How** 每個提示詞以 [主題]、[限制] 等佔位符讓使用者代入，並要求模型逐題訪談、提出反向觀點、給出三套計畫、用白話解釋、校稿、查核事實、精簡或批判文章。

**What** 八個提示詞為 Interview Me、Challenge My Thinking、Help Me Make a Plan、Explain This to Me、Find Errors in My Writing、Help Me Fact-Check、Help Me Tighten My Writing、Roast My Work；其中事實查核、精簡與批判三項由《Der Spiegel》AI 負責人 Ole Reissmann 提供。

### 重點
- Interview Me：要求模型一次只問一題，最後產出可審閱的回答紀錄
- Help Me Fact-Check：把主張標為 HARD 或 SOFT，並加註 SENSITIVE 標出可能有法律風險的內容；原文強調這只是查核起點
- Find Errors in My Writing：要求引用原句列出校稿問題，且明確禁止改寫內容

Build a list of questions and check in with me about them. 3. Then ask one question at a time and wait for my response. 4. Ask thoughtful follow-up questions based on what I share. Adapt the questions as you go.... Use this to: Clarify your own jumbled ideas . Having questions to respond to forces you to organize your thoughts. Get past a blank page. Evade writer’s block one reply at a time. Instead of facing a giant topic, you’re just responding to one small query. Ask for: Probing follow-up questions that push you to go deeper or to be more clear, specific, or vivid in your responses. A summary or transcript of your responses at the end of the interview. Find Errors in My Writing 🔍 Act as a careful copy editor. Review the draft below and give me a numbered punch list of the most important fixes, in order of importance. Check for typos, spelling, punctuation, grammar, awkward syntax, clichés, word repetition, passive voice, weak verbs, jargon, and readability issues. Quote the exact phrase for each issue and explain briefly what's wrong. Don't rewrite anything. Use this to: Clean up a draft before it goes to your editor, client, or readers. Learn how to strengthen your writing by identifying weaknesses. Ask for: Questions when something in your writing is unclear. Feedback from the perspective of a specific audience, like professional peers or newcomers to the field. Ole Reissmann , the first director of AI at Der Spiegel, one of Germany’s top news publications, sent me the following three prompts he finds especially useful. They build on his recent guest post about three free AI tools . 6. Help Me Fact-Check ✅ Ole: Fact-check the text below and give me a checklist for my own review. Find every claim that could be true or false: numbers, dates, names, roles, events, quotes, comparisons, or cause and effect. Split sentences with several claims. Number each claim. List opinions separately as “Not checkable.” Tag each claim: HARD (concrete, directly verifiable) or SOFT (vague or interpretive). Add SENSITIVE if it could have legal implications for a person or business. These tags should describe a claim’s type, not whether or not it’s true... Ask AI to help you verify factual claims. Have it go sentence by sentence, flagging statements it can check with web searches. Consider this a starting point, not comprehensive verification. The example prompt, which you’ll see in the prompt library accompanying this piece, was built with the help of OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 and tested with various models. Advanced version: Teach your chatbot a skill with detailed instructions. Big-if-true is a skill for Claude Code and OpenAI’s Codex. It tells your AI assistant how to identify and verify claims and display the result as a visually compelling HTML file. Caveat : It works best with the most powerful new models. 7. Help Me Tighten My Writing ✂️ Ole: You are a demanding editor. Be strict, but keep my voice. I don’t want to lose my personality. Look for: 1. Redundant sentences that add nothing new. Suggest deleting them. 2. Repetition: places where I make the same point twice, even if I use different words. Suggest which version to keep. 3. Passive voice: suggest an active alternative. 4. Filler words: actually, basically, really, quite, arguably, certainly, somewhat, just, in fact, etc. Rules: - Quote my original exactly, so I can find it. - Don’t add new content. Preserve my meaning, facts, and tone. - Flag only real problems. If a category has no issues, say so... Use this to: Get actionable writing feedback from an AI tool without ceding control of your work. Start with the version in the prompt library , then adjust it with your own rules: What are you looking for when editing text? What specific audience do you have in mind? To what extent do you want your AI to go beyond errors and point out flabby sentences or confusing phrases? Leave a comment 8. Roast My Work 🔥 Ole: Read the text below as a fair-minded, critical colleague. Help me strengthen my argument while preserving its purpose and voice. Be candid, specific, and constructive. Focus on missing counterarguments, cause vs. correlation, sources and interests, and blind spots... Your brutally honest chatbot will rip apart your argument if you ask it to. It will counter your claims with facts and point out weaknesses with blunt candor. Or you can invite it to be a fair-minded, critical but polite colleague. If you ask for a critique, you’ll get one, even if your text is already good. Bots tend to comply, so they rarely say a piece needs no changes. With that in mind, let AI roast your writing. Decide for yourself whether the machine has a point or is just flailing about for anything to criticize.
