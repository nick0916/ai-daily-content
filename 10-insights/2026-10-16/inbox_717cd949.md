## Why Math Dumped OpenAI

作者主張 AI 與數學社群的信任已破裂，並引用 Terence Tao 的「證明消化不良」論點。

**Why** 作者認為 OpenAI 的位置來自預算與聲量，而非實際貢獻，且其與數學界的關係已惡化。

**How** 文章以時間線追蹤 Tao 從 2014 年預言數位化數學、2023 年用 ChatGPT 學習 Lean，到 2026 年提出 proof indigestion 的過程。

**What** Tao 指出證明生成與驗證的自動化快於證明消化，數學進展未同步加速；作者另指 Lean FRO 的投入不到 OpenAI 資源的 1%，並批評 DOGE 期間 NSF 數學經費遭削減。

OpenAI’s position at the frontier of Mathematics is not that of the player making the biggest contribution, but of the player with the highest budget, biggest mouth, and least integrity.

This is Terence Tao, considered the greatest living mathematician. Over the past decade he has also become a cultural leader in the digitization of Mathematics. We will mostly follow him in this timeline. He is a credible spokesperson for their constituency.

He’s pictured above in 2014, the year he said:

One day we may actually write our papers not in LaTeX, but in some language which some smart software will convert to a formal language, and every so often you’ll get a compilation error — the computer does not understand how you derived this step

— Terence Tao

Although there were already programming languages capable of doing this, such as Isabelle , they were considered obscure and hard to use. Tao was being extremely progressive in anticipating they could catch on for working mathematicians.

Today, when we speak of “AI doing Math” at advanced levels, the most common setup is an LLM-based agent driving a traditional proof assistant (Lean) building on a curated library of already formalized results (Mathlib).

For more, see How Terry Tao Became an Evangelist for AI in Math article by Kevin Hartnett or his new book The Proof in the Code about the creation of Lean prover. Highly recommended.

I first heard of Lean in this 2019 Kevin Buzzard talk: “ The Future of Mathematics? ”. He was part of a growing group of Mathematicians supporting the adoption of proof assistants to formalize the results.

He called out a key challenge: to become exciting to the field, we needed to be able to formalize new results . They depend on many old results, not yet formalized — a classic bootstrapping problem. He illustrated this on Fermat’s Last Theorem , the de facto “final boss” of Number Theory results, which Andrew Wiles famously proved in 1995 after it went unsolved for 300 years because it required ideas not yet invented. Formalizing the FLT proof is hard .

Give me 100 million dollars and 10 years and I believe I could get a team together to formalize a proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem. No Mathematician I have met disputes this.

Currently prohibitively expensive.

But worse, no proper mathematician would care [because it’s an old accepted proof].

— Kevin Buzzard, 2019

He eventually got 1M and 5 years, which is something! We’ll come back to that.

In March 2023, in response OpenAI’s release of GPT-4, I wrote the Mechanized Mending Manifesto to model how these innovations relate to our challenges in industrial software. It was clear something had changed, but what? I encouraged thinking of systems in three elements where there were two before.

This was a counter-narrative to the idea that Large Language Models are a direct replacement for people or algorithms. These each have different strengths and weaknesses that we need to understand to build good systems. I did not yet understand that magnitude of the vested interests trying to making LLMs appear more human-equivalent than they actually were. That was naive of me.

I began looking for balanced systems that leveraged the strengths of each element, to show the way.

Later that year I saw these momentous words on social media.

Terence Tao is using ChatGPT to learn Lean prover.

People with context knew this could be huge. If top mathematicians adopted Lean prover it could shape the future of mathematics, as Buzzard predicted. Tao’s leadership could be the push that made the difference. ChatGPT helped reduce the friction while Lean controlled for the notorious LLM-hallucinations.

This was a perfect example of the harmony my manifesto suggested: LLMs, algorithms, and people working together to leverage strengths of each.

The Natural Number Game is still the best way to start learning Lean, and it’s now more self-documenting so you should be fine without a ChatBot if you prefer. See my post How To Learn Theorem Provers .

I declared 2024 The Year of Formal Methods , inspired by Hillel Wayne and Marianne Bellotti. I learned many provers, had many headaches, and made many videos.

To get the best results from LLMs, we need to understand their weaknesses. Unfortunately, I often found people became extremely touchy about LLMs having weaknesses, because they were trying to push the Gold Rush’s manifesto: Embrace AI or be Left Behind. People trying to make LLMs actually work were constantly accused of failing to get on board.

Within a year of ChatGPT’s release, my attempts to advocate balanced solutions had left me so alienated from the “AI-first” business world that I “took back” the term Luddite and vowed to take it as complement and an aspiration.

Terence Tao continued teaching other Mathematicians his formalization methods on his blogs and YouTube videos, using Lean prover along with ChatGPT and Claude Code. He coordinated large collaborative efforts . He gave many talks and interviews about the potential for AI in Math and Science.

He was as “on board” as you could ask anyone to be. Albeit he seems less happy in this picture.

Tao points out the pitfalls as we have optimized some of the process but not others. He explains this as proof digestion. We were generating proofs without integrating them into our understanding. Most of this increased output was not advancing Math .

Perhaps surprisingly, this massive acceleration in proof generation has not actually produced significant acceleration in mathematical progress itself [...]

Just as modern societies no longer consider raw food ingredients as constituting a meal, I predict that mathematical research culture will cease considering “raw”, “undigested” proofs as constituting a solution to a problem, and focus more on how the field as a whole, as opposed to just the problem itself, is enriched by the contribution.
