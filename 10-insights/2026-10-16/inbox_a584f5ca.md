## 3 Free AI Tools From Spiegel’s AI Chief

用語音輸入、補全與個人知識庫，讓 AI 幫忙寫作而不取代思考。

**Why** 作者提出的核心問題是：如何使用 AI 而不讓自己的思考變遲鈍，也不把控制權交給大型科技公司。

**How** 多數推薦工具可在本機以開源模型運作，例如 Cotabby 與 Cotypist 以 Qwen、Gemma 等模型做 Tab 補全，兼顧隱私與速度。

**What** WhisperDictation 是免費的 Mac 語音輸入工具，可加入最多 100 個自訂詞彙；Marker 是瀏覽器編輯器，仍在 2026 年 10 月的 beta 階段；Obsidian 搭配 Claude Code 或 Codex 外掛，並可用 Hister 做本地語意全文搜尋。

### 重點
- WhisperDictation 為免費 Mac 語音輸入，作者稱口述速度至少是打字兩倍，替代方案有 OpenWhispr 與 whisper-local
- Cotypist（付費）與 Cotabby（開源）在 Mac 上以本地模型做逐字 Tab 補全，Windows 替代方案為 Lightkey 與 Typilot
- Obsidian 分三階段：收集網頁、用 obsidian-second-brain 自動整理、用 Miyo 把 vault 接到 Claude Code 或 Codex

Turn Your Voice Into Text Ole: Let&#8217;s start with not writing at all. Dictating is at least twice as fast as typing. That&#8217;s why AI natives would rather speak into a mic than pound at a keyboard. You talk. Your computer listens and transcribes, cleaning up and fixing your copy along the way. Tons of apps offer dictation, many paid, but WhisperDictation (GitHub) is a reliable free one for Mac. It&#8217;s lightning fast and private on your device. How it Works Download the software free. When prompted, download a free language model that works on your device. Designate a trigger on your keyboard, like the option key, as your magic start button. When you press it, the computer starts typing while you talk. (I dictated this paragraph). Customize it: Add up to 100 words, names or places that you frequently use so it will spell them correctly. Coders appreciate the developer vocabulary built in. Alternatives: OpenWhispr (Windows and Mac, free and paid tiers), and whisper-local (Windows and Mac, open source). 2. Writer Faster with Autocomplete Here&#8217;s another way of speeding up typing if you&#8217;re not yet comfortable speaking to your computer: tab completions . It started with code. You write an instruction, your editor suggests the next line, and you press Tab to accept. Now it works for everyday writing too. This can really speed up work. Here&#8217;s how it works: a program watches you type, runs everything through AI, and suggests the next word or phrase. Your typing can finally keep pace with your thinking. Over time, the tools adapt to your writing and finish your sentences in your style. Recommended tools: I&#8217;ve used Cotypist (paid) and Cotabby (open source) on my Mac. With either, you pick a free, open AI model like Qwen or Gemma to download and run locally on your computer. That&#8217;s good for privacy and necessary for speed. After that, it pretty much works out of the box. You can tinker with personalization settings and adjust the length of suggestions. Windows alternatives : Lightkey (paid) or Typilot (paid, all platforms). A related helpful tool: Marker offers another twist on completion. It&#8217;s a browser-based text editor that uses AI differently. You do the writing. It answers questions. One of its first features: filling in gaps. Say you&#8217;re writing about the new Apple CEO and forget his name. Just type &#8220;xxx&#8221; instead. That&#8217;s a signal for Marker to fire up its AI in the background and guess what you&#8217;re looking for. It suggests John Ternus, based on Apple&#8217;s site. Marker is still in beta as of October 2026. 3. Set Up Your Personal Archive A &#8220;second brain,&#8221; sometimes called a Zettelkasten , is a searchable collection of your notes and readings. It gives you ready access to valuable context as a supplement to your memory. The benefit: Save notes or articles easily and later use AI to quickly surface relevant material and identify subtle connections across what you&#8217;ve saved. The challenge: Setting up this kind of system used to require rigorous organization. AI to the rescue! Using your new system: Let&#8217;s say I&#8217;m in a meeting with Jeremy. I can ask my archive questions like: What previous notes do I have on this topic? What are my five latest tasks? What thoughts have I written up about AI agents? Start with Obsidian : Begin with this free, clean text editor (featured in a prior Wonder Tools post ). It stores simple files in a vault, which is really just a folder that lives on your computer. For sync and backup you can use OneDrive, Google Drive, or iCloud. Collect what&#8217;s useful: Add the Web Clipper browser extension to save articles in one click. Add a community plugin like Copilot or Claudian to use Claude Code or Codex as your AI collaborator. Level 2. Add AI to help organize. Use a plugin like obsidian-second-brain to automatically organize your notes and your vault. Caveat: This takes a bit of learning. Level 3. Connect your archive to an AI assistant. Make your notes available to a chatbot so it can search them when you ask. Miyo links Obsidian vaults and other folders to Claude Code or Codex (ChatGPT). Bonus: Search What You&#8217;ve Read Hister gives you a searchable full-text archive rather than simple bookmarks. The good news: It&#8217;s free and runs privately on your computer. It can search by meaning, not just keywords. The bad news: It can be intimidating to set up. But these days, if you get stuck, just ask AI for help.
