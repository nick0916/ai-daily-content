## NaiveAI Promises 2,100 Tokens per Second. How Well Does It Code?

測試一個主打每秒生成超多 token 的開源寫程式模型，看它速度快是否真的實用。

**Why** 研究與工程需要快速反覆實驗，所以 NaiveAI 以生成速度為首要目標。

**How** 以小米 MiMo-V2.5（310B 參數、15B 啟用的 MoE）為基礎，再訓練約 3.25T token 改為 DeepSeek Sparse Attention，並搭配自家 NaiveRT 推論引擎與推測解碼（speculative decoding）drafter。

**What** Naive-N0.5-Flash 對比 DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 與 GLM 5.3 Flash：中位回應時間 3.1 秒，對 5.3 秒與 10.1 秒；平均時間 45.3 秒，與 DeepSeek 的 45.0 秒相近，困難題則較慢（89.8 秒對 79.7 秒）。三個 app 測試中，開思考時太陽系最佳、樹模型與 DeepSeek 並列、光線追蹤次於 DeepSeek；關閉思考時樹模型無法產出可用 app。目標價為輸入 $0.10 / 輸出 $0.40 / 快取讀取 $0.01 每百萬 token。樣本僅每難度 10 題且未評分正確率，速度數據來源也不同（伺服器端對 OpenRouter），不能直接當倍數比較。

### 重點
- 30 題測試中，Naive-N0.5-Flash（思考）中位回應 3.1 秒，優於 GLM 5.3 Flash（high）5.3 秒與 DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017（high）10.1 秒；平均則與 DeepSeek 相近（45.3 對 45.0 秒）
- HLE 難題平均完成 token 約 108,500，約為 GLM 的 9 倍、DeepSeek 的 11 倍；整體中位數約 3,200 對平均逾 51,000，少數極長推理拉高均值
- 官方 benchmark：NL2Repo-Bench 71.9（高於兩個對手）、DeepSWE v1.1 67.8、Agent's Last Exam 32.4（介於兩者之間）；目標價 $0.10 / $0.40 / $0.01 每百萬 token

Editor’s note: With this post, we are launching a new series focused on trying new models and products with unbiased reviews. Reach out with suggestions to tips@latent.space. Naive-N0.5-Flash is a new open-weights model built for AI coding tasks and tuned to generate as many tokens per second as possible . It was developed by a Chinese company called NaiveAI , with the aim of speeding up engineering and research workflows so that teams can experiment, iterate and solve complex problems faster. The model is an evolution of Xiaomi’s MiMo-V2.5 , which NaiveAI has trained further for long-context reasoning and paired with its own NaiveRT inference engine. Naive-N0.5-Flash arrives with a bold pitch: a model built largely by AI and trained to do AI research itself, with recursive self-improvement as the long-term goal. To see what changed, it helps to know where it started. MiMo-V2.5 is a 310B-parameter sparse Mixture-of-Experts model with 15B active parameters, trained on 48T tokens. Its language backbone inherits the hybrid design of MiMo-V2-Flash, which mixes sliding-window attention (SWA) with global self-attention. NaiveAI's recipe starts from those weights. Through continued, multi-stage training, the team adapted the checkpoint to DeepSeek Sparse Attention layers. According to the NaiveAI technical blog , this took roughly 3.25T additional tokens on top of MiMo's original 48T. About 3T of those additional tokens were trained at a fixed 1M context length and with a constant learning rate. Benchmarking NaiveAI vs. DeepSeek and Z.ai We benchmarked the ultrafast mode of Naive-N0.5-Flash against DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 and GLM 5.3 Flash , and then gave all three the same app-building prompts. The trade-off, we discovered, is that Naive-N0.5-Flash takes longer to reason through harder problems, yet it still delivers answers faster overall. However, that comes at a slightly higher cost per question — although that may be an acceptable price for applications where speed comes first. Most popular models on OpenRouter as of the end of October 2026. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 and GLM 5.3 Flash are two of the most popular models in OpenRouter . Both belong to the Flash model class and demonstrate excellent capabilities in AI-assisted coding . Speed and verbosity experiments Speed is the headline feature. 1 NaiveAI promises faster research iteration than comparable models, which matters to developers and AI researchers who need to test ideas quickly. 2 We evaluated each model on 10 randomly sampled, text-only questions at each of three difficulty levels: 10 easy questions from MATH-500 , 10 medium questions from GPQA-Diamond and 10 hard questions from HLE-Diamond . That makes 30 questions per model. All experiments used the same maximum completion-token limit of 256K. For each request, we recorded response speed (tokens per second, TPS), the total number of completion tokens, and end-to-end response time. The code for the experiments is at https://github.com/gbeltramo/llm-verbosity . The tables below report the mean and median across all 30 successful requests for each configuration. Throughput figures come from different sources: OpenRouter for DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 and GLM 5.3 Flash, and server-side measurements for Naive-N0.5-Flash. They should be compared with that in mind. Graphic by Gabriele Beltramo Key observations Naive-N0.5-Flash reports exceptionally high throughput , approaching or passing 1,000 tokens per second in both thinking configurations. Because its figures are server-side and the other models' figures come from OpenRouter, we do not claim an exact speed multiple over DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 or GLM 5.3 Flash. Thinking has a large effect on verbosity. On the hard HLE questions, Naive-N0.5-Flash with thinking on averaged about 108,500 completion tokens, roughly nine times the GLM 5.3 Flash (high) average and 11 times the DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) average. The medians tell a different story from the means. Across all 30 questions, the median completion for Naive-N0.5-Flash with thinking on was about 3,200 tokens, far below its average of more than 51,000. A small number of requests produced exceptionally long reasoning traces and pulled the average up. The same pattern appears, less sharply, in the other models. The tables also include end-to-end response time, which is the fairer basis for judging speed. The median question took 3.1 seconds with Naive-N0.5-Flash (thinking), against 5.3 seconds for GLM 5.3 Flash (high) and 10.1 seconds for DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high). The means are closer: 45.3 seconds for Naive-N0.5-Flash, 45.0 for DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017, and 86.9 for GLM 5.3 Flash. On the hard questions, Naive-N0.5-Flash (thinking) was slower on average than DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) , at 89.8 seconds against 79.7. Two caveats apply. The sample is small, at 10 questions per difficulty level, and we did not grade answer accuracy. Short, fast replies are only useful if they are correct. App experiments We also asked the models to build the same web apps from the same initial prompt. We tested six combinations of model and thinking effort, with the thinking effort in parentheses: Naive-N0.5-Flash (no thinking) Naive-N0.5-Flash (thinking) DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (low) DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) GLM 5.3 Flash (low) GLM 5.3 Flash (high) The gallery of apps is available online. In summary, the three prompts asked the six model variants to do the following: Create an interactive 3D view of the Earth and the Solar System. Grow a 3D tree from a seeded random generator, with branches that taper and fork, thousands of leaves, and bark and leaf textures. Do a browser port of the book Ray Tracing in One Weekend . Viewers can orbit the camera around the scene and watch the planets circle the sun, with controls to speed up or slow down their motion. Every surface is drawn from generated textures, with no image files. Naive-N0.5-Flash (thinking) produced the best result of the three. Its sun shows sunspots, and the dark side of its Earth glows with city lights at night. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) came close behind. Its version lacks an asteroid belt, and its Earth has less textural detail. GLM 5.3 Flash (high) stumbled on lighting, which makes the planets hard to tell apart. Its speed controls are also too sensitive, so a small nudge sends the planets racing or crawling. Tree modeling The second app grows a 3D tree from a seeded random generator, with branches that taper and fork, thousands of leaves, and bark and leaf textures. Wind makes the branches sway and the leaves flutter, and the shadows on the grassy ground move with them. The tree stands under a sky gradient and light fog. A control panel adjusts the seed, branching depth, leaf count, wind strength and direction, and sun position. There’s also a switch to autumn colors and a frame-rate counter sits in the corner. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) and Naive-N0.5-Flash (thinking) both produced good results. The surprise was Naive-N0.5-Flash with thinking switched off , which did not produce a usable app at all. The good results still had small glitches in the wind animation. In the DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) app, thin branches can detach from their parents in strong wind and appear to float. The effect is subtle, but it is there. The problem is easier to see in the GLM 5.3 Flash (high) app. When the wind intensity is turned up, the whole tree starts to deform and wobble like a jelly sculpture in an earthquake. Ray Tracing in One Weekend The third app is a browser port of the book Ray Tracing in One Weekend . It renders the book's cover scene: a vast gray ground, a scatter of small matte, metallic and glass spheres, and three large spheres in the center. One is glass, one a brown matte and one a polished metal. Depth of field blurs the foreground and background. The image builds up progressively on screen, with a progress bar, elapsed time and a rays-per-second estimate. A menu offers the book's earlier milestone scenes. The user can pick a quality preset and download the result as a PNG. In this more coding-focused scenario, all three models with thinking on produced a working app. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) wins because its ray tracer is the most efficient of the three. It also uses the full width of the screen to display the rendered image. The Naive-N0.5-Flash (thinking) app is correct, but its JavaScript is less efficient. GLM 5.3 Flash (high) is again the weakest. Its speed is similar to the Naive-N0.5-Flash app, but its interface is more basic. Pricing Naive positions N0.5-Flash as a capable, low-cost model. The target prices are $0.10 per million input tokens, $0.40 per million output tokens, and $0.01 per million cache-read tokens . That puts it in the $0.20 to $0.60 per million output tokens band occupied by several capable Flash models, including the two we compared it against. The final bill will also depend on how many tokens the model uses. That in turn depends on the thinking budget requested and the difficulty of the questions. Takeaways In ultrafast mode, Naive-N0.5-Flash can peak at around 2,000 tokens per second and sustain nearly 1,000 during thinking-heavy tasks. In our tests, its median response time was several times shorter than those of GLM 5.3 Flash and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 at their high thinking settings. Mean times were similar to DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017. On complex tasks, it spent far more thinking tokens than the other two models. On easy and medium questions, it consistently replied faster. In the apps, Naive-N0.5-Flash was much more consistent than GLM 5.3 Flash (high). With thinking on, it also holds its own against DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017. It built the most detailed solar system app, tied with DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 on the tree model, and came a close second on the efficiency of the ray tracer.
