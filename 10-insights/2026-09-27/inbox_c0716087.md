## I tested Meta Muse vs ChatGPT-6 with 5 prompts — OpenAI just lost

用五個日常提示比較 Meta Muse 與 ChatGPT-6，Muse 以 3 比 2 勝出。

**Why** Meta 推出新模型，作者想看它在日常任務上的表現，而不只看基準測試。

**How** 作者以五個提示測試寫作、規劃、解決問題、解釋與創意推理，再逐項判定勝負。

**What** Muse 在鄰居來信語氣、給 10 歲孩子解釋飛機原理、設計新 app 三項勝出；ChatGPT-6 在有預算與交通限制的紐約行程、以及時間緊迫的待客規劃兩項勝出。作者仍以 ChatGPT 為主要工具。

### 重點
- Muse 勝出的項目重視語氣與對象理解，例如鄰居來信不會顯得被動攻擊
- ChatGPT-6 在有明確限制的任務較佳，例如紐約一日遊需符合 200 美元預算與避開長距離步行
- 測試僅五個提示、由單一作者判定，樣本小，文章結論為 Muse「3 比 2 勝出」

Meta has a new AI model, and I wanted to know how it handles the kinds of prompts people actually use, especially in comparison to ChatGPT-6 . Meta Muse is the company’s latest model, arriving at a time when ChatGPT has become the default chatbot for millions of people . But benchmarks only tell us so much. What matters more to me is whether an AI can write the awkward text you’ve been avoiding, plan a realistic day out with your family or explain something complicated without making it even more confusing. So I put Meta Muse and ChatGPT through five everyday prompts covering writing, planning, problem-solving, explaining and creative reasoning. I wanted to know which one best understood what I was actually asking for and which could give me the best answer to use After five rounds, there was a clear winner — but the results were much closer than I expected. 1. Handling an awkward situation (Image credit: Future) Prompt: My neighbor keeps putting their trash cans partly in front of my driveway, and it's making it difficult to back out. Write a text asking them to stop. I want to be friendly because we get along, but clear enough that they actually change what they're doing. Don't make it sound passive-aggressive. ChatGPT led directly with the request, cutting to the chase and delivered a note that eliminated ambiguity around how far over they need to go. Meta delivered a courteous and natural note. The ask feels neighborly and the phrasing suggests a minor physical adjustment rather than something more serious. Winner: Meta wins for a draft that hits the prompt’s tone constraints better for an ongoing, friendly relationship. ChatGPT’s message of “completely clear” reads like an HOA notice and slightly passive-aggressive. 2. Planning under constraints (Image credit: Future) Prompt: Plan a Saturday in New York City for a family with three kids ages 5, 9 and 12. We have a budget of $200 for activities and food, want to avoid spending the whole day walking, and need at least one indoor activity in case it rains. Give me a realistic schedule from 10 a.m. to 7 p.m . ChatGPT did a nice job with geographic clustering, to ensure the walking distance was short for the first part of the day. The zero-cost options and pacing of the day with high energy and sit-down segments were also helpful. Meta picked an attraction with far higher entertainment value for the targeted age spread (5, 9, and 12). The American Museum of Natural History (AMNH) easily captivates all three ages simultaneously. Meta also demonstrated accurate NYC transit knowledge by explicitly citing the MTA’s 44-inch height cutoff for free child rides, included a practical playground stop right across the street, and gave concrete logistical guidance (e.g., sitting on the right side of the ferry outbound). Winner: ChatGPT wins. While Meta's AMNH plan is more fun on paper, its entire budget math hinges on an unstated assumption of tri-state residency. ChatGPT wins on strict adherence to the prompt's financial and logistical constraints. 3. Advice / problem-solving (Image credit: Future) Prompt: I have 45 minutes before guests arrive. My kitchen is messy, the living room needs vacuuming, there's laundry on the couch and I still need to shower. Give me the most efficient plan to get everything done. Tell me what I should skip if I run out of time. ChatGPT anticipated a critical blind spot the prompt forgot to mention: the guest bathroom. Adding a 5-minute block to check toilet paper and wipe the sink is the difference between a good plan and a realistic one. It also provided highly specific guardrails for the shower block, which prevents the most common reason people run late in this scenario. Meta delivered behavioral advice, which was excellent, and explicitly stated the rationale for the timeline and ending with the mandate to use a timer. It’s timing format was also easier to read than ChatGPT’s. Winner: ChatGPT wins for operational realism and anticipating unstated needs. While Meta's timer advice is sharp, its kitchen expectations are a setup for failure. ChatGPT recognized that when you have 45 minutes, you are mostly straightening and organizing, not necessarily cleaning it in such a short amount of time. 4. Explain something complicated simply (Image credit: Future) Prompt: Explain why airplanes can stay in the air to a curious 10-year-old. Don't use equations or assume they know what lift, drag or air pressure mean. Keep it under 200 words and use an analogy they could picture. ChatGPT introduced an accurate, intuitive physical mechanism: wings push air down , which pushes the plane up (Newton’s third law translated into plain English). Meta offered perfect audience calibration. The tone is lively and speaks directly to a 10-year-old ("with a grown-up driving, of course!", "whoosh"). It picked one analogy—the hand out the car window—and committed to it entirely, tying it back to paper airplanes and the runway to explain why forward speed matters. Winner: Meta wins . It kept the energy high, matched a 10-year-old's reading level without talking down to them and delivered a clear, memorable takeaway. 5. Creative reasoning (Image credit: Future) Prompt: Invent a new app that solves a small but annoying everyday problem people have at home. It can't be a to-do list, meal planner, budgeting app or smart-home remote. Give it a name, explain the problem it solves, describe how it works and tell me why someone would actually keep using it after the first week. ChatGPT solved a universal human frustration without requiring whole-house cataloging. The interaction model involves snapping a photo and speaking natural language ( "Christmas lights in the blue bin" ) is low-effort. Meta identified a hyper-specific, universally relatable domestic failure point and gave the app a brilliant, punchy name ( Takeout ). Its retention logic is rock-solid: instead of pretending users love opening chore apps, it acknowledged that the best utility apps require zero maintenance and rely on loss aversion. Winner: Meta wins because the chatbot built a product around the reality of household laziness and recognized that an app survives past week one only when it demands almost no manual input from the user. Final thoughts After five prompts, Meta Muse takes the win 3-2. To say I'm both shocked and impressed is a serious understatement. While ChatGPT was stronger when a prompt required careful planning and attention to practical constraints, Meta Muse stood out when the task depended more on understanding people. Its neighbor text sounded like something I'd actually send, its airplane explanation was perfectly pitched for a 10-year-old and its app idea showed a surprisingly good understanding of why people abandon apps that require too much effort. Meta Muse feels very much like it's own model, not one that's trying to imitate or catch up to ChatGPT. The little "Muse" at the top is unnecessary, but adorable. ChatGPT is still my go-to, but this test proves that Meta has come a long way and this latest model offers a pretty strong first impression.
