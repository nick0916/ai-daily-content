## Selling an AI SaaS Platform After 200 Buyers Called It Crazy

先保留完整平台，但讓客戶從單一工具切入，比要求整套替換容易成交。

**Why** 要客戶拆掉整套 Salesforce 門檻太高，首次購買的承諾過大。

**How** Reevo 把功能歸為 pipeline 開發、交易推進、主管報表三個切入點，讓客戶從最痛的一項開始，並以 Mom Test 式非引導問題驗證需求。

**What** 創辦人 David Zhu 表示 Reevo 隱形開發兩年，靠 AWS 計畫中的一位創辦人成為首位付費客戶，6 個月達 $1M ARR；由 HubSpot 出身的創始銷售主管完成成交。

### 重點
- 架構保持一體化平台，但不要求整套替換；客戶可先只買 dialer、sequencer 或 AI-native CRM 其中一項
- 把功能分成三個切入點，讓客戶選最痛的一項，而不是一次面對所有模組與整合
- 早期無 RevOps 主管、無資安審查的新創全套採用屬假性驗證；真正目標是有既有系統的中型企業買家

He asked buyers to rip out their whole Salesforce stack. The first 200 to 300 called him crazy. David Zhu, co-founder and CEO of Reevo, built an all-in-one AI sales platform and learned the hard way how to sell it. He kept the platform and changed the ask: buyers start with one piece, like the dialer, the sequencer or the AI-native CRM. 

 David explains why Reevo spent two years in stealth before selling, how a founder he met at an AWS program became the first paying customer before seeing the product, and how Reevo got to $1M ARR in 6 months while an introvert ran founder-led sales. He also shares the Mom Test mistake he made early on. 

 Plus: why the startups that happily adopted his whole platform turned out to be false validation. 

 David ran engineering teams at DoorDash and worked with Keith Rabois at OpenStore before founding Reevo, his fifth startup, with a founding team of 14. Reevo now serves hundreds of customers without paid marketing. 

 🔑 Key Lessons 
 
 Be strict on architecture, flexible on the sale: Reevo kept its all-in-one platform but stopped demanding rip-and-replace. Letting a buyer start with one tool asks for a much smaller first yes than throwing out Salesforce. 
 Give buyers three doors, not the whole menu: Reevo groups its land motions into pipeline generation, deal progression and manager reporting. Prospects pick their biggest pain instead of facing every module and integration at once. 
 Watch out for false validation: Startups with no RevOps leader or security review adopted Reevo's whole platform easily. That said little about the entrenched mid-market buyers Reevo actually wanted to win. 
 Play to your strength, not someone else's game: David had no sales brand, so he built on what he did have, a dense engineering team. Copying founders who have distribution you lack is how you lose. 
 Open the deal, then hand off the close: David, an introvert, sent the first email and ran the intro call. A founding sales leader from HubSpot closed, and Reevo copied his talk tracks across 12 to 15 AEs. 
 Ask for payment to test your discovery: Early prospects praised David's pitch, then said no when he asked them to pay. The Mom Test's non-leading questions get you to the honest answer sooner. 
 Don't outsource ICP discovery: Paying a vendor $50,000 to run your outbound skips the learning. David says founders without product-market fit have to do discovery themselves or risk a false start.
