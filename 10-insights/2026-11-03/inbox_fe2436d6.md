## What an AI-Native Product Team Actually Looks Like at Salesforce

AI 產品需要持續重建與監控，所以開發流程要從一次性的 SDLC 改成不斷循環的 ADLC。

**Why** 每隔幾個月就有新模型出現，既有 agent 要隨之調整甚至簡化；另外 agent 會發生 drift，例如退款政策文件被改寫但程式沒變，導致退款金額錯誤。

**How** ADLC 把流程改為 observe、build、test、evaluate、deploy 的持續循環，並偵測 context 變化、在 production 修正。Salesforce 的六步做法：讓團隊自選工具、以 Slack 為作業系統、用 Agent Script 讓關鍵答案具確定性、先建 eval、把第一線回饋接到 roadmap、先做一個成功案例再全面推廣。

**What** Aakash Gupta 在 Dreamforce 訪談 Salesforce AI 工程負責人 Jayesh Govindarajan，談他如何重塑約 700 人的組織。他的 PM 篩選標準依序為 agency、agent-native 心態、懷疑精神、craftsmanship、能定義何謂好；文中稱 Salesforce PM 薪資範圍為 19.4 萬至 48.7 萬美元。

### 重點
- ADLC：以 observe、build、test、evaluate、deploy 持續循環取代線性 SDLC；每個 AI 功能都要持續重建，應先為 evals 與 observability 編列預算再編列開發預算
- Agent Script 用於標出 agent 中必須有確定性答案的部分（例如不能出現兩個不同的 pipeline 數字）；eval-first 指在信任 agent 前先決定如何評分，通用 evals 不夠，需要領域專業
- 團隊導入六步：自選工具（團隊自行由 Cursor 轉向 Claude Code）、Slack 當作業系統、確定性 agent、eval first、回饋接 roadmap、單點成功再擴大

Should I do more like this? Watch the full masterclass ↓ Apple | Spotify | YouTube 1. Why the SDLC should be replaced with an ADLC We’ve all lived the SDLC , software development lifecycle. For most of us, it looks something like plan it, build it, test it, ship it, look at the results. The big change with AI is that every few months, a new model ships with new capabilities. For the past couple years, that’s meant a lot of progress on long-running agentic tasks . What this means for product builders is that you need to extend the capabilities of the agent you built . You need to tune how it serves the existing capabilities, and you need to keep doing that. Jayesh noticed that his teams would go back and rework every agent they built, often simplifying it because the new model can do more on its own. That’s pretty different from prior tech we built on. Did you ever have to dedicate sprints to SQL getting an upgrade? It didn’t happen often, right? And so that’s why we need a new software development lifecycle, which Jayesh calls the ADLC : The reason you have to keep rebuilding the agent is because of drift . The agent works. For instance, an agent answers refund questions grounded on the refund policy doc. But then someone rewrites the doc, the update isn’t clear, and the code doesn’t change. As a result, the refunds come out too big. That’s a big problem. So then you have to detect it. You have to figure out what context changed, and whether it changed the right way or the wrong way. Then you have to correct it in production . So the ADLC is built to accommodate that. We go from a linear process to a continual iteration . Of course, we all sort of had a continual iteration, but here Jayesh is just codifying it. We go from observe, build, test, evaluate, deploy. And so now we just continuously expect to rebuild our products . That’s also why we’re so picky about what we put into our product , because we know it’s going to have a high maintenance cost when it’s an AI feature. We’re going to spend a ton of money, time and energy on evaluating and observing that well. So evals become incredibly important, and we need to embrace that continuous shipping methodology . Every AI feature you ship is one you’ll keep rebuilding. Budget for evals and observability before you budget for the build. 2. The path to building an AI-native product team at Salesforce scale One of the things I consistently see from my readers is that you guys are having to work on building AI-native product teams . You’re being asked to do that. You’re the type of person who reads this newsletter. So I spent a lot of time with Jayesh understanding how he has rebuilt how 700 people work inside of Salesforce. We broke the path down into 6 steps: Step 1. Give teams a choice of tools This is one of the most important takeaways I had from our conversation: execs shouldn’t pick the winner. The goal for product leaders is to let teams choose. Do they want to use Codex ? Do they want to use Claude Code ? Do they want to use Cursor? That’s how Jayesh’s teams moved from Cursor to Claude Code without a mandate. As he watches the performance of the best, he helps others learn from that. But he is enabling all tools. Step 2. Make Slack the operating system I think this is something a lot of people attempting this sort of tooling shift miss. You want to find a tool where you are always available asynchronously. For many people that is Slack. Slack can become the home for your company OS Slack bot. It can even become the home for user feedback from your customers , with Slack Connect. In Slack, PMs can get feedback on a prototype directly from a customer. Step 3. Make the agent deterministic One of the things you’ll encounter when you build these is there are some points where you don’t want to have two answers. In the case of Salesforce’s agents, for instance, they do not want to have two numbers for pipeline. You can’t have one say 25 million and one 20 million. That’s going to be a big problem. So they use something called Agent Script, which allows them to identify which parts of the agent should have deterministic answers . This was a really interesting nuance he brought up that I think other builders might not have. It points to the importance of getting this question answered: Where should I be using LLM AI? When should I be using determinism inside of my AI feature ? Step 4. Become eval first Jayesh told the story of how his data science and engineering teams differed. Data science teams were already eval native. Because they came up through the era of predictive AI, they always expected a model to be wrong some of the time and wrote tests against outcomes . But most of his engineers, on the other hand, came from the deterministic world, where a test passes or fails. Those are the teams us PMs need to help become eval first. What it means to be eval first is that you decide how you’ll grade the agent before you trust it. Essentially, each eval checks something. And generic evals really don’t cut it. So as a PM, you develop or bring in the subject matter expertise to really win in the domain you’re delivering an agent for. Step 5. Wire feedback to the roadmap For Jayesh’s org, this means that every forward deployed engineer posts to one Slack channel. Each post is tagged as a paper cut or a feature request, and PMs match it against the roadmap. When they do build something specific for a customer, they ask, should we be building this for everyone? This allows you to learn directly from the field in order to improve your product. Step 6. Roll out one win, then go wide This is the classic pilot advice, but I was asking Jayesh where teams go wrong, and he brought it up as super important. You have to find that area where you are going to show value. Once you do that, then you go wall to wall with this sort of an ADLC instead of your normal SDLC. 3. What Salesforce looks for when hiring PMs I’ve written about this a lot here. AI PMs have two skills that a regular PM doesn’t: AI prototyping and AI evals . Jayesh agreed that those are the two most important, but when he’s screening , here’s what he’s looking for first: 1. Agency This is actually the thing Jayesh finds in short supply, and I probably agree when hiring on my own team. You want people who are high agency, and so that’s what he looks for first. 2. An agent-native mindset Are you the type of person who, after doing a task a few times by hand, then teaches an agent to do it? That’s what he’s looking for. 3. Skepticism You want to love the power of AI, but at the same time, you can’t believe everything. This means you’re checking every output before you’re shipping it. 4. Craftsmanship Craftsmanship applies to the product as much as the docs. You need to be able to sign your name on absolutely everything that lives in the product and the docs that you send out. In particular, Jayesh finds red flags in people who send prototypes that look like every other prototype, or long docs that are just sent as a checkbox. 5. Setting the bar This is probably the most important area for PMs, always. Can you say what good looks like? Because if you can, that’s how you set up the evals and the test cases that we talked about being so important. A good PM is an AI PM who understands AI prototyping and AI evals and brings these five character traits.
