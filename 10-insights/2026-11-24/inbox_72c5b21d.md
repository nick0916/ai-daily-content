## GPT 6.2 Astra Solves a Historical Mystery

用 GPT-6.2 Astra 做歷史研究，22 分鐘內找出 1808 年無名素描簿的作者。

**How** 只給極少提示，模型自行查閱公開的一手與二手資料，找到紙本檔案中冷門的來源，並預測它能驗證假設；它也找到日記中 vicomte de Chabot 的訪問紀錄。

**What** 史學家 Mark Humphries 的案例：素描簿藏於 Yale Beinecke Library；文中認為 Chabot 的旅行日誌（需向 Cleveland State University 訂購）可進一步確認作者身分，Humphries 並認為這個新來源本身對加拿大與毛皮貿易史研究者也很重要。

### 重點
- 作者為 Yale Beinecke Library 藏 1808 年無名素描簿，GPT-6.2 Astra 在 22 分鐘內完成推論
- 模型找到日記中 vicomte de Chabot 的訪問紀錄，建議其旅行日誌可用來確認作者身分

GPT 6.2 Astra Solves a Historical Mystery . Here's an interesting case-study in using GPT-6.2 Astra for historical research, from history professor Mark Humphries:

With only minimal prompting, GPT-6.2 Astra was able to autonomously identify the creator of an anonymous 1808 sketchbook held at Yale’s Beinecke Library . It did so extremely quickly (in just 22 minutes), using publicly available primary and secondary sources. To do so, it discovered a little known source in a paper archive, predicted that it would prove its hypothesis if examined and, in the end, it did. Not only that, but this new source turns out to be a major find on its own that will be of significant interest to both Canadian and fur trade historians.

Here's the digitized sketchbook .

Astra found a diary reference reference to a visit by the vicomte de Chabot dated five days before a date mentioned in the sketchbook at a nearby river, and suggested that Chabot's journal of his travels (only available by order from Cleveland State University) might confirm that the sketchbook was his work.

Are historians due for a round of Deep Blue ? Mark thinks it will be mild:

But if the experience of mathematics is any guide, we can also anticipate that sometime next spring or summer we’ll face a sort of crisis of confidence as we struggle to understand how to respond to agentic systems that can do things human historians cannot. To be fair, that crisis will almost certainly be less profound for us than it is for math. The problem in mathematics, of course, is that solving hard math problems was the whole game. Historians, on the other hand, constantly reinterpret the past in light of an evolving present; we don’t chase a single universal truth. We also (in the ideal at least) write books and articles that are closer to works of philosophy and literature than they are to scientific tracts. Humanity is thus something of a requirement for the production of historical literature.
